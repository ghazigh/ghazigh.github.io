§ 01
Profile

Who am I, really?

A short self-portrait — the research that trained my instincts, and the production work that keeps them honest.

I build AI agents that actually finish the job. Day-to-day I'm Principal Agentic AI Developer at CDPQ, one of the world's largest institutional investors, where I design and ship multi-agent platforms: planner–executor–critic architectures, retrieval-augmented backbones, Model Context Protocol (MCP) servers and evaluation harnesses — the pieces that let LLM agents plan, use tools and complete multi-step workflows reliably, at the scale and risk tolerance of a financial institution.

The reason I can do that quickly is the decade behind it. I recently defended my Ph.D. at ÉTS Montréal under Prof. Georges Kaddoum — Excellent mention for both thesis and defense, and a jury nomination for the institutional Excellence Award. My dissertation, Multimodal Collaborative MARL for 6G V2X Digital Twins, was fundamentally about the same problem agentic AI faces: how do several autonomous agents, each with a partial and noisy view of the world, coordinate well enough to act correctly together?

“

Most agent projects don't fail on the model. They fail on context, on tools, and on the absence of a way to measure whether the thing works.

That is where I spend my time with clients: choosing the smallest architecture that solves the problem, wiring agents to real systems through well-designed tools rather than brittle prompts, and building the evaluation harness first so improvement is measurable instead of anecdotal. I've done the full arc — visiting researcher at UC Irvine on adversarial robustness, Mitacs-funded deep learning for property valuation, NLP engineer on a clinical chatbot, graph-partitioning ML for FPGA emulation at Mentor Graphics — so I respect the distance between a notebook and a deployment, and I enjoy crossing it.

I also care deeply about how knowledge travels. I teach at ÉTS, mentor students and junior engineers, and write about what I learn. Clarity-first isn't a slogan — it's the only way I know to make complex AI useful to the people paying for it.

§ 02
The practice

What I build for clients.

Six things I do repeatedly, and have done end-to-end — from the first architecture sketch to the on-call rotation after launch.

P-01 Architecture

Multi-agent systems

Planner–executor–critic topologies, supervisor/worker graphs, state and memory design, agent-to-agent handoffs, and the human-in-the-loop checkpoints that make autonomy acceptable to a risk committee. I start by arguing for the smallest architecture that solves the problem — most "multi-agent" briefs turn out to need one good agent and three excellent tools.

  • LangGraph · orchestration
  • Planner / critic loops
  • HITL checkpoints
P-02 Context

Retrieval & knowledge

The unglamorous 70% of every agent project. Ingestion pipelines, chunking strategies that survive contact with real documents, hybrid and semantic search, knowledge graphs, freshness and versioning, plus retrieval-quality metrics so "the answers got better" becomes a number instead of a feeling.

  • RAG pipelines
  • Embeddings · hybrid search
  • Knowledge graphs
P-03 Integration

Tools & MCP servers

An agent is only as capable as the tools you give it. I design tool surfaces that models actually use correctly — tight schemas, honest error messages, idempotency, permission boundaries — and expose your internal systems through Model Context Protocol servers so any agent, today's or next year's, can reach them safely.

  • MCP servers
  • Tool & schema design
  • Systems integration
P-04 Trust

Evaluation & guardrails

My research background was built on measuring things people preferred to assert — my bachelor thesis was on adversarial robustness, and it stuck. I build the eval harness before the demo: golden datasets, task-level and trajectory-level scoring, LLM-as-judge with human calibration, regression gates in CI, tracing and cost/latency budgets in production.

  • Eval harnesses
  • Tracing · observability
  • Guardrails · red-teaming
P-05 Delivery

Prototype → production

The part most pilots never survive. Containerized services, CI/CD, secret and identity management, background workers for long-running agent runs, structured logging, cost controls, and a deploy path your team can own after I leave. I've shipped on Azure, AWS and GCP — and I write the runbook.

  • FastAPI · Docker · CI/CD
  • Azure · AWS · GCP
  • Runbooks · handover
P-06 Frontier

Applied research

When the off-the-shelf answer doesn't exist. Multimodal perception and sensor fusion, reinforcement and multi-agent RL, vision transformers, digital twins and simulation harnesses for training and stress-testing policies. Four peer-reviewed IEEE publications, and a habit of turning a hard paper into a working prototype.

  • Multimodal · fusion
  • RL · multi-agent RL
  • Digital twins · simulation

§ 04
Engagements

How we'd work together.

Four shapes of engagement. Most clients start small — an audit or a prototype — and expand once there's something working to point at. Scope and pricing are set per project.

E-01

Agent Readiness Audit

1–2 weeks

Before anyone writes an agent, find out where agents would actually pay off. I review your workflows, data estate, tooling and constraints, then come back with a ranked shortlist of candidate use cases, an honest read on data readiness, the architecture each one needs, and the risks that would sink it.

  • Ranked opportunity map with effort / impact
  • Data & tooling readiness assessment
  • Reference architecture + build plan
E-02

Prototype Sprint

2–4 weeks

One real agent, on your data, calling your tools — not a demo on synthetic examples. You end the sprint with something your team can use and your stakeholders can judge, plus the evaluation harness that tells you whether it's good enough to go further.

  • Working agent against real systems
  • Eval harness + baseline scores
  • Go / no-go recommendation with cost model
E-03

Production Build

6–12 weeks

Taking a validated prototype all the way to something you can put in front of customers or regulators: hardened architecture, retrieval and tool layers, guardrails, observability, CI/CD with regression gates, and a handover that leaves your engineers owning the system rather than depending on me.

  • Production-hardened multi-agent system
  • Guardrails, tracing, cost & latency budgets
  • CI/CD, runbook, team handover
E-04

Advisory & enablement

Ongoing

For teams already building who want a senior second opinion. Architecture reviews, eval strategy, model and vendor selection, hiring input, and hands-on workshops that leave your engineers able to do this without me. Typically a fixed number of days per month.

  • Architecture & code review
  • Evaluation strategy design
  • Workshops & team enablement
How I work —
  • Evals before demos. If we can't measure it, we're guessing.
  • Smallest architecture that works. Complexity is a cost you pay every month.
  • Ship in weeks, not quarters. Something real in front of users early.
  • You own the outcome. Code, runbooks and knowledge stay with your team.

§ 06
Bibliography

Peer-reviewed publications.

For the freshest list see my Google Scholar profile.

  1. [P-04] 2025

    Multimodal Collaborative Perception for Dynamic Channel Prediction in 6G V2X Networks.

    G. Gharsallah & G. Kaddoum
    IEEE Transactions on Machine Learning in Communications and Networking

    Shows that fusing camera, LiDAR and radar across cooperating vehicles — rather than relying on single-sensor views — substantially improves short-horizon wireless channel prediction under real V2X mobility.

  2. [P-03] 2025

    Multimodal Collaborative Perception for 6G V2X Power and Spectrum Management.

    G. Gharsallah & G. Kaddoum
    ICC 2025 — IEEE International Conference on Communications

    Closes the loop between perception and radio resource management: collaborative multimodal features drive a joint power-and-spectrum policy that outperforms channel-only baselines in throughput and interference.

  3. [P-02] 2024

    MVX-ViT: Multimodal Collaborative Perception for 6G V2X Network Management Decisions Using Vision Transformer.

    G. Gharsallah & G. Kaddoum
    IEEE Open Journal of the Computer Society

    Introduces a Vision-Transformer architecture that treats heterogeneous V2X sensors as a unified token stream, learning end-to-end network-management decisions directly from raw multimodal evidence.

  4. [P-01] 2023

    ViT-LoS V2X: Vision Transformers for Environment-Aware LoS Blockage Prediction for 6G Vehicular Networks.

    G. Gharsallah & G. Kaddoum
    IEEE Access

    First work to bring Vision Transformers to line-of-sight / blockage prediction for 6G vehicular links, demonstrating that spatial attention on the urban scene generalises better than CNNs across intersections and weather.

  5. [B-00] 2020

    AI Classifiers' Robustness to Adversarial Attacks — Manipulating Output of Classifiers by Small Input Perturbations.

    G. Gharsallah & Z. Wang
    Bachelor thesis · UC Irvine, supervised by Prof. Zhiying Wang

    Foundational study that shaped how I think about AI evaluation today: how imperceptible input perturbations flip classifier decisions, and what it takes to measure — not just claim — robustness.
